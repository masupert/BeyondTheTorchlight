# 13.0 - Running the Game

# The Basics
## Establish a Safe Playing Environment
The game can create uncomfortable situations and may contain episodes of horror due to the nature of the play at the table. Before the start of the game, there should be a discussion with everyone playing to establish what should be expected and what is tolerated at the table. 

Players and characters should not be shamed in any way for how they look, play, speak, dress or participate in the game. This is expected of every player at the table. 

- Remember you are a player too and your fun matters just as much as everyone else’s. 

The number one rule for anyone is to make sure that the other players the table are having fun first. 

For additional information please visit: https://ttrpgsafetytoolkit.com

## Listen and Let Creativity Rule
Your role as the GM is to set the scene, and let the players react and respond. 

- Make sure everyone has a chance to be heard and act in a scene. 
- Beyond the Torchlight does not have rules for every thing that may happen, and it even has less rules than many mechanics that other systems have. Let your players come up with creative and fun ways to use the things they find in the world. 

## Find the Right Details to Focus On
Every tabletop RPG utilizes game mechanics to generalize certain aspects of the world while focusing on the details of other aspects. *Beyond the Torchlight* is designed to focus on the dangers and perils of adventuring. 

Some aspects of the game are very detailed while other aspects are generalized. For example, inventory slots and coin matter a lot, but hours in the day or distances in feet don’t matter quite so much. 

There are many areas in a survival adventure where too many details can get in the way or slow the game down. Focus on the immersion of the game by translating the mechanics you and the players enact into narrative elements. 

- Use coin to track and manage all resources.  Track construction projects or improvements to the town through cost not time. 
- Utilize time in broad swaths and focus on *Phases*. 
- Don’t focus on specific feet or distances when adventuring or delving. 

# Fleshing out the Town
## Town Upkeep
As players continue to adventure they will need to find reason for their characters to continue to want to head out into a dangerous and wild world. Coin is an obvious way to force players into the wild. 

A town sitting on the edge of the wilderness will need resources to keep itself operating. A typical town with four players will have at a minimum of 28 characters.

The monthly upkeep for a town is 500 coin per month or approximately 18 coin per character per month. Increase the monthly cost by 200 coin when the town reaches 30 and 50 characters.  

Characters will also need to spend coin on their monthly living expenses, businesses, etc. These costs should range from 10-25 coin per month depending upon the profession and if the player is investing in the character, such as building a new blacksmith forge. 

Cost for investments in the town or in a character’s personal life can be track usually with a coin tracker. A larg investment for the town, such as a palisade may cost 1000-2000 coin.

## Tiers of the World Map
The world map can be divided into tiers of place based upon distance from the town. The further the distance players are from their home base, the higher the risk of enemies and the greater the rewards of treasure.

### Treasure vs. Coin
When adapting adventures from other games, magic items should generally be treated as Treasure. These are known as Artifacts. Other large monetary values should be converted into “Treasure”. Treasure does not have a specific monetary value to coin but is incredibly useful for improvements for the town or obtaining other finished goods from the civilized world. 


# At the Table
## Guidance on Rolling Dice
- Player’s choices at the table and interacting with each other are more important than rolling dice to succeed.
- Try to keep rolling only for situations when the stakes matter, the outcome is uncertain, or the players may be hurt.
- Apply Enhancements and Impairments to provide or restrict information without rolling. 
- Failed rolls do not need to mean that the player wasn’t successful in their attempt. It just means that there was a consequence of their attempt. 
- There are no critical successes or failures, but you can have a degree of success or failure if you choose. 
## Make Rulings
- The rules in the game are fairly light, but even with the existing rules, the GM has the leeway to make rulings based upon the specific situation if it furthers the story or fun at the table. Rulings should be consistently applied to the game going forward or be declared that it is an exception for this specific situation and explained why. 
- Ask the players for their input on a ruling if it changes any of the existing rules in the game. 
## Dealing with Player Deaths
- Let players know that if they fail they may die with a skill check. 
- Player deaths don’t always have to be meaningful, but make sure you acknowledge the death at the game table. 
	- Encourage players to have a moment to react to a death when it happens. 


## Tips for Encounters in the Wild
Violent encounters grind the game to a halt and should be used sparingly. 
Utilize other hazards for encounters such as environmental, social, resource scarcity, puzzles, unknown magic.
